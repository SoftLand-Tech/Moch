Already running hermes

One command on your Linux machine. moch-link never installs, configures or modifies hermes — it connects your install to the official relay and prints the QR.

curl -fsSL https://moch.softland.tech/install.sh | bash
  • Sessions, providers and skills are already there

Starting fresh

No hermes yet? Install the agent itself (one command, from the hermes project) — then link it with the same line as above.

curl -fsSL https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/install.sh | bash
  • Then: curl -fsSL https://moch.softland.tech/install.sh | bash
  • The real hermes — the same agent upstream, nothing forked or wrapped
  • Works on Linux, macOS, WSL2 and Termux

Both end the same way: a QR on your screen, a scan, and your agent in your pocket.

Open source, end to end

Moch is three MIT pieces — the app, the link, and the relay that connects them. Every one of them can run on hardware you own.

Run the whole stack yourself

The app is open source, moch-link is one script, and the relay is a single Node file you can host anywhere. Self-host the relay and there is no third party in the loop at all — not even us.

  • The relay's source and deploy guide — same code ours runs
  • Point moch-link at it with MOCH_LINK_RELAY=wss://your-relay …
  • Your machines, your keys, your rules — MIT, forever

Or use ours — free while Moch is new

Don't want to host anything? The official relay at api.moch.softland.tech is the default, and it's free for everyone right now, so you can just try Moch.

  • No account, no signup, nothing to configure — it's already in the QR
  • It routes by token hash and stores no traffic
  • Self-hosting stays free forever — that's the point of open source

Why not just message a bot on Telegram?

Honest question. The Hermes gateway even ships a Telegram bot, and it's a fine way to start. Here's what changes when the phone gets an app of its own.

A bot inside a chat app

Your messages ride a platform on the way home.

WHERE MESSAGES GOPhone, then the chat platform's servers, then your agent.
WHAT AN ANSWER LOOKS LIKEPlain text in a bubble.
WHEN IT NEEDS PERMISSIONYou answer in a plain text reply.
CHANGING A SCHEDULED JOBType the right slash command, syntax and all.
WHAT IT COSTS YOUAn account and a bot token on someone else's platform.

Moch

A direct line to your own machine.

WHERE MESSAGES GOFrom your phone to your computer. The relay, if you use one, just passes the call along — it stores nothing but a token hash.
WHAT AN ANSWER LOOKS LIKEThinking you can open, code that's formatted, lists that are lists.
WHEN IT NEEDS PERMISSIONA card arrives. Tap Allow or Deny.
CHANGING A SCHEDULED JOBAn Automations screen with switches, not syntax.
WHAT IT COSTS YOUNothing. Your device, your server, that's the whole relationship.

Both are front doors to the same agent, and you can happily run them side by side. Moch is for when your phone deserves better than a text box.
